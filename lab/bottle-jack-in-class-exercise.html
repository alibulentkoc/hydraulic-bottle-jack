Given

  1. Rated load W = 4000 lbf, printed on the jack
  2. Lifting cylinder bore D_r = 2.00 in
  3. Pump cylinder bore d_p = 0.50 in
  4. Pump piston stroke s_p = 0.70 in
  5. Lifting cylinder extension L = 7.00 in
  6. Pivot to pump rod end a = 1.00 in, pivot to operator hand b = 12.00 in
  7. Handle swept angle theta = 44.4 deg, measured with an angle gauge
  8. Reservoir annulus, not shown: inside diameter D_i = 2.10 in, outside diameter D_o = 3.50 in, oil column height H = 6.00 in
  9. Pumping rate n = 1 full cycle per second. Use 1 hp = 6600 in-lb/s.

Calculate

  1. Pump piston area A_p and lifting cylinder area A_r.
  2. Volume displaced per full pump stroke, V_p = A_p s_p.
  3. Lift produced per stroke, h = V_p / A_r. State which principle this uses.
  4. Pressure in the lifting cylinder at the rated load, P = W / A_r.
  5. Force on the pump piston, F_p = P A_p. State which principle lets you use the same pressure on both pistons.
  6. Torque at the handle pivot, T = F_p a, and the force at the operator's hand, F_hand = T / b.
  7. Number of strokes and the time to reach full extension, then the speed of extension.
  8. Hydraulic power P Q, output linear power W v, and operator power from the arc the hand travels, F_hand (2 pi b theta / 360) n. Convert each to horsepower.
  9. Reservoir annulus area, the drop in oil level at full extension, and whether the reservoir holds enough oil for one full extension.
  10. Force multiplication W / F_hand. Show that it equals the area ratio times the lever ratio.

Check in the simulation

Switch the units to Customary and enter the eleven values from the list above. The Laboratory calculations panel then works the same quantities, in nearly the same order. Mark any answer that differs from yours and find the reason before moving on.

Explore

  1. Predict, then change the lifting cylinder bore to 4.00 in. What happens to the lift per stroke, the number of strokes, and the hand force? Explain all three from one principle.
  2. Restore 2.00 in, then halve the pump bore to 0.25 in. Predict the hand force before you look.
  3. Your three power figures should be one number for an ideal machine. Which of the three differs, by what factor, and why? The panel shows the factor.
